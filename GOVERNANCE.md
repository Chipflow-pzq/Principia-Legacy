# Governance and contribution policy

The founding maintainer manages releases. Scientific authority does not follow from this role. Contributors may propose corrections, document disputed interpretations, or supply better sources. The corpus preserves attribution, uncertainty, and disagreements; it does not infer consensus from model agreement.

Public contributions should use a patch with the exact base record hash, a reason, the next revision, and source anchors. Report a suspected source or attribution error using the issue template. New or corrected substantive content remains a candidate until screened. Changes affecting support quarantine direct interpretations for recheck; they do not automatically negate all downstream science.

Human reviews require an explicit reviewer-authored attestation. Editorial source checking and domain-expert assessment are separate scopes. Reviewers should state relevant expertise and conflicts of interest. Agents must never manufacture human attestations or impersonate reviewers. A local attestation records an assertion of identity, not cryptographic proof of identity.

The first release is exploratory. Specialist review, multilingual expansion, independently maintained packs, and stronger publisher authentication are future work. Model screening, library tests, and synthetic evaluation scores are not substitutes for expert judgment.

Contributions of code use MIT; original editorial writing uses CC BY 4.0. Submit only material you are entitled to contribute. Source-specific rights survive every export. No external publishing, outbound messages, or autonomous spending is authorized by corpus content.
